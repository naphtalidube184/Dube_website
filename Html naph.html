About

I’m a Mining Engineer 1 at Freeport-McMoRan’s Safford Operations, an open-pit copper mine in Arizona. My day-to-day work is slope monitoring with radar and prisms, checking that pit designs were built as planned, and keeping the pit surface model up to date.

Before Freeport I spent two years at Bravura Zimbabwe on an underground platinum project on the Great Dyke, working across feasibility studies, environmental assessment, ore control and mine design.

I hold an M.S. in Mining Engineering from Missouri University of Science and Technology. My research uses machine learning for mining decisions, and I like to test every design against the numbers: cutoff grades, cash flow and NPV.

I’m open to geotechnical and mine planning consulting work, especially on projects in southern Africa.

    Breakeven cutoff if the metal price changes

    Breakeven cutoff = (mining + processing + G&A) ÷ [(price − selling cost) × recovery × payable × unit factor]. Unit factor is 22.046 lb per tonne per 1% for copper, and 1 ÷ 31.1035 oz per gram for gold and platinum. Costs are per metric tonne.

    Stripping ratio check

    Compares the planned stripping ratio with the most waste one tonne of ore can pay to remove. If the planned ratio is higher, that part of the pit loses money.

    –
    Planned stripping ratio (waste : ore)
    –
    Breakeven stripping ratio
    –
    Profit per tonne ore
    –
    Total undiscounted profit

    Breakeven stripping ratio = (value per tonne ore − ore mining − processing and G&A) ÷ waste mining cost.

    Planar sliding factor of safety

    Checks a single bench or slope for sliding on one joint or fault plane, using the Hoek and Bray limit equilibrium method. Assumes a flat crest and no tension crack. Water is set with a pore pressure ratio.

    –
    Factor of safety
    –
    Sliding block weight (kN per m of wall)
    –
    Sliding plane length (m)
    –
    Can the block slide?

    FoS = [c·A + (W cos ψp − U) tan φ] ÷ (W sin ψp), with W = ½ γ H² (cot ψp − cot ψf), A = H ÷ sin ψp and U = ru · W cos ψp. Typical bench design targets FoS ≥ 1.1 to 1.3, depending on the operation’s acceptance criteria.
